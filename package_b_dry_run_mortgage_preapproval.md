# Package B dry run: Digital mortgage pre-approval journey

Internal note: This is an internal dry run showing how the current Package B skill set can work together on one realistic scenario. The example is illustrative, not client-specific. It is written to test whether the workflow produces a clear concept direction, a usable future-state journey, and a practical delivery recommendation before scope is locked.

## Scenario

**Client type:** Mid-size retail bank with online banking and advisor-led mortgage sales

**Problem area:** Prospective home buyers begin a digital mortgage pre-approval journey, but many drop out before they understand affordability, document requirements, or what happens next, which pushes them into the call center or leaves advisors with poorly prepared leads

**Decision to support:** What mortgage pre-approval concept should the bank move into delivery first, and what should be built now, deferred, or validated further?

**Why this fits Package B:** The opportunity area is already known, but the bank still needs evidence on user needs, the future-state flow, and the supporting service changes before delivery scope and priorities can be set with confidence

## Activity 1.1 using `scoping-workshop-facilitator`

### Workshop objective

Agree the journey in scope, business case, success criteria, and the discovery questions the package must answer.

### Draft workshop output

**Journey in scope:** Digital mortgage pre-approval from first affordability exploration through advisor handoff

**Business case:** Increase the number of qualified mortgage leads, reduce avoidable contact-center load, and improve advisor efficiency by helping customers arrive better prepared and with clearer intent

**Outcomes to influence:**

* Increase progression from affordability exploration into a completed pre-approval start
* Improve the quality of leads entering advisor conversations
* Reduce clarification calls about eligibility, documents, and next steps

**Success measures:**

* Increase start-to-completion rate for the pre-approval journey
* Reduce calls related to document requirements and process uncertainty
* Increase the share of advisor meetings where the customer arrives with the necessary details prepared
* Reduce drop-off between affordability estimation and advisor booking

**Scope boundaries:**

* In scope: early mortgage pre-approval and advisor handoff
* Out of scope: full mortgage underwriting, loan offer generation, and post-approval case handling
* In scope for service implications: document guidance, advisor context handoff, and clarification of what can happen digitally versus with an advisor

**Priority discovery questions:**

* What makes customers stop before continuing from affordability estimation into pre-approval?
* What trust, clarity, or effort barriers shape the decision to continue?
* What needs to happen digitally before an advisor handoff feels worthwhile?
* Which supporting process, content, ownership, or data changes are needed behind the flow?

## Activity 1.2 using `user-research-runner`

### Research objective

Understand what customers are trying to achieve in the mortgage pre-approval journey, where they hesitate, and what shapes their decision to continue or abandon.

### Sources reviewed before interviews

* Web analytics from the mortgage funnel
* Call-center topics related to mortgage eligibility and document preparation
* Advisor input on lead quality and common meeting breakdowns
* Existing feedback from first-time buyers and remortgage customers

### Participants represented

* First-time buyers exploring affordability
* Existing homeowners considering a move
* Customers who started but did not finish the pre-approval journey
* Customers who booked an advisor instead of using the digital flow

### Top user needs

* A plain-language understanding of what pre-approval actually means
* A realistic view of affordability before investing more time
* Clarity on which documents or information are needed and when
* Confidence that starting digitally will not damage their position or waste time

### Main barriers

* Users cannot tell whether the flow gives a real answer or only a rough estimate
* The document and information burden feels larger than expected
* Customers worry about credit impact, hidden checks, or being pushed too early into a sales conversation
* The handoff between digital flow and advisor support is unclear

### Decision criteria

* Trust in the guidance
* Effort required to continue
* Confidence in what happens next
* Sense that the bank already understands their situation when they do speak to an advisor

### Implication for later work

The concept should not focus only on a calculator or form. It needs to create a clearer progression from early confidence-building, into readiness, into a better-prepared advisor handoff.

## Activity 2.1 using `current-journey-synthesizer`

### Mapping objective

Map the current mortgage pre-approval journey to show where the experience and the supporting service break down across users, advisors, and internal systems.

### Current-state journey summary

**Journey stages in scope:**

* Explore affordability
* Decide whether to continue
* Enter personal and financial details
* Understand requirements and next steps
* Book or request advisor support

**Users, teams, and systems involved:**

* Prospective customer
* Mortgage advisor
* Contact-center agent
* Mortgage lead system
* Online banking and public web journey
* CRM and meeting-booking tools

### Main breakdowns

1. **The meaning of pre-approval is unclear early in the journey**
   Why it matters: Customers do not know whether they are getting a reliable indication, which weakens confidence before they invest effort.

2. **The effort required to continue appears suddenly**
   Why it matters: Document and information needs feel like a jump in commitment, which pushes users to abandon or call instead.

3. **The digital flow and advisor handoff feel disconnected**
   Why it matters: Customers do not trust that the time spent digitally will help them later, and advisors often receive weak or incomplete context.

4. **Internal teams and systems create repeated clarification work**
   Why it matters: Contact-center agents and advisors spend time explaining what the journey is, what the estimate means, and what needs to happen next.

5. **Trust breaks before value is obvious**
   Why it matters: Customers face uncertainty and jargon before the journey proves why continuing is worth their effort.

### Opportunity areas and decision criteria

* Clarify the promise and limits of pre-approval earlier
* Break the effort into a more guided progression
* Improve the quality of the digital-to-advisor handoff
* Reduce internal clarification and duplicate explanation work

The later concept should be judged on trust, clarity, preparedness, and reduced service friction.

## Activity 3.1 using `future-state-concept-facilitator`

### Concept objective

Define a future-state mortgage pre-approval journey and the service changes required to make it work in practice.

### Experience principles

1. **Build confidence before asking for commitment**
   Why it matters: Users need to understand the value of continuing before they invest effort.

2. **Show progress and readiness clearly**
   Why it matters: The journey should help users see what they know, what is missing, and what the next step will be.

3. **Make advisor handoff cumulative, not a restart**
   Why it matters: Customers and staff should feel that digital effort improves the later conversation rather than duplicating it.

### Future-state journey summary

* Start with a clearer affordability and readiness view in plain language
* Introduce a guided pre-approval path that explains what the bank can indicate now and what still requires advisor confirmation
* Add a staged checklist for financial details and document readiness
* Create a handoff summary that carries customer inputs, questions, and readiness signals into the advisor conversation

### Required service changes

**Process**

* Define a clearer threshold for when the journey should stay digital and when advisor input is needed
* Tighten the handoff between digital lead capture and advisor preparation

**Content**

* Rewrite the explanation of pre-approval, affordability, and next steps
* Replace jargon-heavy requirement descriptions with a clearer readiness checklist

**Ownership**

* Clarify which team owns the digital pre-approval experience versus the advisor handoff
* Define who keeps the guidance and checklist content accurate

**Data**

* Ensure the advisor receives the customer’s pre-approval inputs and readiness context
* Capture progression signals so drop-off and lead quality can be measured more precisely

### Open concept questions

* How much customer detail should be captured before the handoff starts to feel too demanding?
* Which parts of the readiness checklist can be introduced before login or account creation?

## Activity 4.1 using `prototype-and-prioritization-facilitator`

### Prototype objective

Make the future-state mortgage pre-approval concept tangible enough for stakeholder review and use it to decide what should be delivered first.

### Clickable core-flow brief

**Core flow to illustrate:**

1. Affordability and pre-approval explanation
2. Readiness check and guided next-step view
3. Advisor handoff summary and booking step

**What the prototype must show:**

* A clearer explanation of what the customer gets at this stage
* A staged view of what information is needed and why
* A visible handoff into advisor support with context preserved

### Prioritization matrix summary

**Build first:**

* Clear pre-approval promise and limits in plain language
* Guided readiness checklist
* Advisor handoff summary with captured context

**Defer:**

* Full document-upload orchestration
* Deep integration of income and asset data sources
* Broader redesign of the full mortgage sales journey

**Validate next:**

* Whether customers trust the staged readiness approach enough to continue
* Whether advisors can use the handoff summary without extra manual clarification
* Whether the concept improves progression without raising drop-off at the data-entry stage

### Delivery recommendation

Move forward with a first delivery phase that improves clarity, readiness, and handoff quality before investing in broader automation. The concept appears strong enough to justify bounded delivery, but the bank should still validate the handoff and effort curve before committing to deeper integration work.

## What this dry run shows

This Package B example shows that the current skill set can now support a full service concept definition flow:

* The scoping workshop creates a usable business and journey frame
* The interview stream sharpens real user needs and decision criteria
* The current-state journey map shows where experience and service friction reinforce one another
* The future-state concept session turns the insight into a practical target journey plus service changes
* The prototype and prioritization step turns that concept into a delivery-facing recommendation
