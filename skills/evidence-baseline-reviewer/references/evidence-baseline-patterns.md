# Evidence baseline patterns

Use this reference to turn existing evidence into a practical baseline for Package A.

## Contents

* Review objective
* Recommended review flow

* Known-versus-assumed framing

## Review objective

The purpose of the evidence baseline is to give the sprint a grounded starting point.

By the end of the review, the team should have enough clarity on:

* Which signals already appear strongest
* Which points are reasonably well supported
* Which points are still assumption
* Which gaps or conflicts matter most before later recommendation work

## Recommended review flow

### 1. Reconfirm the decision and problem focus

Start by restating:

* The selected problem area
* The business question the sprint must support
* The users or staff groups that matter most

### 2. Review the available source mix

Check:

* Which sources exist
* How current they are
* Whether they are behavior, opinion, operational, or mixed evidence
* Whether they are broad enough or too partial for the question at hand

### 3. Pull out the strongest repeated signals

Look for:

* Friction points that show up across more than one source
* Signals with clear user, service, or business consequences
* Problems that can guide what the sprint should examine next

### 4. Separate known versus assumed

Distinguish:

* Well-supported points
* Directional signals that are useful but not yet proven
* Assumptions still driving the discussion

### 5. Identify conflicts, gaps, and next validation questions

Capture:

* Where sources disagree
* Where the evidence base is weak or outdated
* Which unanswered question matters most to the later decision

## What to emphasize by source type

### Analytics

Emphasize:

* Where people drop off, hesitate, or repeat work
* Which steps or channels show the most friction

### Support issues

Emphasize:

* Repeat complaints
* Points of confusion
* Service load caused by the experience

### Sales input

Emphasize:

* Objections, hesitation, or lost momentum
* Commercial consequences linked to the experience

### Prior research

Emphasize:

* Existing needs, barriers, and decision criteria
* Findings that still appear relevant to the current question

## Known-versus-assumed framing

Use these buckets:

* Well supported: repeated across credible sources
* Directional: useful signal, but still needs validation
* Assumption: currently belief-driven rather than evidence-backed
* Conflict: sources point in different directions and should be explored further

## Common watchouts

* The review becomes a broad evidence dump instead of a baseline for one decision
* Old or weak evidence is treated as settled truth
* The team confuses interpretation with direct evidence
* One loud internal source outweighs stronger cross-source signals
* Gaps are hidden because the team wants certainty too early
