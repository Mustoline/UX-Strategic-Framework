# Scoping Workshop Facilitator

Use this skill for Package B Activity `1.1`: the 2-hour scoping workshop that sets the boundaries and business logic for the rest of the Service concept definition work.

This skill should help the team agree the journey in scope, the business case, the outcomes the work should influence, the success measures, the scope boundaries, and the questions the discovery work must answer before delivery scope can be set with confidence.

This skill should build on the helper layer:

* Use [workshop-design-kit](../workshop-design-kit/SKILL.md) for agenda, facilitation structure, and playback logic
* Use [evidence-ingest](../evidence-ingest/SKILL.md) if existing evidence needs to be normalized before or after the workshop
* Use [artifact-writer](../artifact-writer/SKILL.md) to draft the scoping output cleanly
* Use [ux-strategy-reviewer](../ux-strategy-reviewer/SKILL.md) as the final quality check on client-facing outputs

## What this skill should produce

For most requests, produce a scoping workshop pack with:

* A workshop objective tied to the business case and the decisions ahead
* A timed agenda for a 2-hour session
* Critical stakeholder questions and follow-up probes
* A note structure focused on journey scope, business case, success measures, scope boundaries, and discovery questions
* A workshop summary that captures the aligned scope and the research frame

## Operating modes

### Prep

Before the workshop, confirm:

* The opportunity area, product, portal, or service journey under discussion
* Which business or delivery decisions this discovery phase must support
* Who will attend and what authority they hold
* What evidence, strategy material, or prior assumptions already exist
* Which parts of the journey seem likely to be in scope and out of scope

Then:

* Use `workshop-design-kit` to shape the 2-hour scoping session
* Use the patterns in [scoping-workshop-patterns.md](references/scoping-workshop-patterns.md)
* Prepare the output structure in [scoping-workshop-templates.md](references/scoping-workshop-templates.md)

### Run

During the workshop:

* Keep the discussion tied to the journey in scope and the decisions this phase must support
* Clarify the business case in practical terms rather than broad aspiration
* Push the group to define scope boundaries clearly
* Turn vague success goals into measures or observable signals
* Surface disagreements on priorities, user groups, and scope assumptions
* End with a short playback of the agreed scope, main success measures, and open discovery questions

### Synthesize

After the workshop:

* Draft a concise scoping workshop summary
* Capture the journey in scope, business case, outcomes to influence, success measures, boundaries, and priority discovery questions clearly
* Show what the later research should now focus on
* If needed, hand the draft to `artifact-writer` and then to `ux-strategy-reviewer`

## Core workshop objectives

The scoping workshop should answer:

* Which journey, product, or service area is actually in scope?
* What business case or commercial logic makes this work worth doing now?
* Which outcomes should the work influence?
* What would count as success for this phase?
* Which questions must the research answer before delivery scope and priorities can be set?

## Question selection

Read [scoping-workshop-patterns.md](references/scoping-workshop-patterns.md) for:

* The recommended 2-hour flow
* Critical questions by section
* Follow-up probes when stakeholder answers stay too vague
* Watchouts that commonly weaken the scoping output

## Output templates

Use [scoping-workshop-templates.md](references/scoping-workshop-templates.md) for:

* Scoping workshop agenda
* Business case summary
* Journey-in-scope statement
* Success-measure template
* Scope-boundary template
* Discovery-question list
* Workshop summary

Adapt the output to the workshop objective rather than filling every section mechanically.

## Facilitation rules

* Treat the workshop as a scope-and-decision framing session, not a generic kickoff
* Keep the conversation at journey and service level, not only at feature level
* Ask for business consequences when stakeholders describe opportunity in abstract terms
* Make in-scope and out-of-scope areas explicit
* If the group lacks enough evidence to settle something, turn it into a discovery question rather than a false agreement
* Prefer a usable research frame over an exhaustive debate about every possible issue

## Minimum output standard

Unless the user asks for something else, return these sections:

1. Workshop objective
2. Suggested 2-hour agenda
3. Critical scoping questions
4. Note structure
5. Draft scoping workshop summary
6. Open discovery questions and risks

## Boundaries

This skill covers Activity `B1.1` only. It frames the scope and business case for Package B. It does not replace the later user research, current-journey synthesis, concept definition, or prioritization work.
