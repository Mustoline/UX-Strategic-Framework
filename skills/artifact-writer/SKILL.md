# Artifact Writer

Use this skill to turn synthesis outputs into clear, commercially grounded discovery artifacts.

This skill is responsible for drafting, structuring, and tightening the written output. It should not invent findings, inflate confidence, or hide evidence gaps.

## What this skill should produce

For most requests, produce a draft artifact that:

* States the business problem or decision clearly
* Explains why it matters now
* Shows the strongest evidence or rationale
* Makes the implications for scope, prioritization, delivery, or investment explicit
* Ends with a practical next step

## Operating modes

### Prep

Before drafting, confirm:

* Which package and activity this supports
* Which artifact type is needed
* Who the audience is
* What decision the document must support
* Which notes, evidence, or prior outputs are available

If the user has not specified the artifact type, choose the one that best fits the decision and state the assumption.

### Run

While drafting:

* Use short sections and direct headings
* Keep the writing commercially grounded and concrete
* Separate evidence, interpretation, recommendation, and open risk
* Avoid design jargon, hype, or vague consultant language
* Make the next step visible rather than implied

### Synthesize

After drafting:

* Tighten the wording so the main message is obvious on a quick read
* Remove repetition and low-value filler
* Check that the document supports a real decision, not just a summary
* Leave the output ready for `ux-strategy-reviewer`

## Tone and writing rules

* Write for a Danish business audience: direct, pragmatic, and commercially grounded
* Emphasize ROI, risk reduction, prioritization, delivery consequences, and practical next steps
* Avoid presenting UX as design activity for its own sake
* Prefer tangible deliverables and concrete implications over abstract claims
* If the evidence is thin, say so clearly and frame the next validation step

## Minimum output standard

Unless the user asks for something else, return these sections:

1. Context or objective
2. What matters most
3. Evidence or rationale
4. Decision, implication, or recommendation
5. Risks, assumptions, or open questions
6. Next step

## Boundaries

This skill does not replace the activity-facing skill that generates the substance of the work. It is the writing layer that turns that substance into a usable internal or client-facing document.
