# Roadmap and business-case patterns

Use this reference to structure the Package C roadmap and business-case step.

## Contents

* Recommended flow

## Objective

The purpose of this step is to turn the validated target-state service model into a practical sequence of change and a credible investment story.

By the end of the work, the team should have enough clarity on:

* What should happen now, next, and later
* Which dependencies and decision points shape the sequence
* Which operating-model, governance, and ownership changes are required
* Which value areas matter most to the investment case
* What the most credible ROI hypothesis looks like at this stage

## Recommended flow

### 1. Reconfirm the target-state direction and decision frame

Start by restating:

* The validated target-state model in scope
* The strategic or investment decision this step must support
* The main feasibility signals, risks, and dependencies already identified

### 2. Shape the phased roadmap

Clarify:

* What should happen now
* What should happen next
* What should happen later
* Which actions are delivery moves and which are operating-model changes

### 3. Map dependencies and decision points

Capture:

* Dependencies across teams, systems, data, or operations
* Decision points that could block or unlock progress
* Ownership or governance issues that affect the sequence

### 4. Summarize change implications

Describe:

* What must change in operating model, governance, or ownership
* Which teams or roles will need to work differently
* Where change effort is likely to be concentrated

### 5. Frame the ROI hypothesis

Connect the direction to:

* Adoption or completion improvement
* Efficiency or effort reduction
* Support reduction
* Revenue or retention impact
* Risk or waste reduction

Use the strongest available signals and name where the logic is still directional.

### 6. Close with the investment narrative and next steps

Summarize:

* Why this sequence makes sense
* What decision should be made now
* What needs to happen immediately after this phase

## What the roadmap must contain

At minimum, capture:

* A now, next, later sequence
* Major dependencies and decision points
* Change implications across teams, systems, or governance
* The main value areas behind the ROI hypothesis
* Risks or assumptions that still matter

## ROI-hypothesis framing

The ROI hypothesis should:

* Use plain language rather than finance jargon
* Link the service change to a small number of credible outcome areas
* Make clear which parts of the case are evidence-backed and which remain directional
* Support an investment conversation without pretending to be a full financial model

Treat this as business-case framing, not spreadsheet forecasting.

## Common watchouts

* The roadmap becomes a wish list rather than a sequence
* Dependencies are implied but not made explicit
* Change implications are underplayed because they are politically difficult
* ROI language becomes vague and generic
* The business case overpromises certainty the work cannot support
* The next decision is not stated clearly enough for senior stakeholders
