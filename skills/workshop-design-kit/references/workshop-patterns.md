## Alignment and decision pattern

Use for sponsor workshops and scoping workshops where the team needs a shared decision frame before broader discovery starts.

### Best for

* Package A sponsor workshop
* Package B scoping workshop

### Focus

* The decision to support
* The problem worth solving
* The users or journeys in scope
* Success measures
* Constraints and known risks

### Default structure

* Opening and objective framing
* Current context and evidence review
* Decision and scope discussion
* Success measures and constraints
* Open questions and next steps

### Critical prompts

* What decision must be easier after this work?
* Which users or internal teams matter most here?
* What would count as progress in business terms?
* What constraints are fixed, and which are assumptions?
* What should this work explicitly not try to solve?

## Mapping pattern

Use for journey-step and current-state mapping sessions where the team needs a shared view of the real current experience.

### Best for

* Package A journey-step mapping
* Package B current-journey work

### Focus

* Actors and touchpoints
* Sequence of steps
* Friction points
* Dependencies
* Moments where time, confidence, or momentum is lost

### Default structure

* Scope the slice of the journey
* Map the current flow step by step
* Identify breakdowns and dependencies
* Rank the most important friction points
* Summarize implications

### Critical prompts

* What actually happens today, not what should happen?
* Where do users or staff get stuck, hesitate, or drop off?
* Which handoffs or systems create friction?
* Which issues create the most delay, confusion, or internal effort?

## Comparison and prioritization pattern

Use for option reviews and prioritization sessions where the team needs a grounded choice rather than a broad brainstorm.

### Best for

* Package A option review
* Package B prioritization workshop

### Focus

* Decision criteria
* Option comparison
* Tradeoffs
* Risks and dependencies
* Build-now versus defer logic

### Default structure

* Confirm the decision and comparison criteria
* Review each option against the criteria
* Discuss tradeoffs and delivery implications
* Rank or score the options
* Agree the preferred direction and the open questions

### Critical prompts

* Which option best supports the intended outcome?
* What delivery risk does each option create?
* What are we choosing not to do if we pick this path?
* What still needs validation before implementation starts?

## Concept definition pattern

Use for future-state working sessions where the group needs to define how the experience should work and what must change behind the scenes.

### Best for

* Package B concept session
* Package C future-state model session

### Focus

* Future-state journey or service logic
* Experience principles
* Process, content, ownership, and data implications
* Feasibility signals

### Default structure

* Reconfirm the main findings from current-state work
* Define the future-state flow or model
* Identify supporting service changes
* Test for feasibility and dependencies
* Summarize the target direction

### Critical prompts

* What should be different in the future experience?
* What needs to change behind the interface for this to work?
* Which ownership, process, or data changes are required?
* Which parts of the concept are still assumptions?

## Ecosystem alignment pattern

Use for half-day workshops where the team needs to agree the service landscape and scope across departments, touchpoints, and systems.

### Best for

* Package C ecosystem workshop

### Focus

* Service boundaries
* Departments and actors
* Touchpoints and channels
* Systems and dependencies
* Critical service moments to study

### Default structure

* Frame the service question
* Identify the main actors and touchpoints
* Map systems and operational dependencies
* Agree service scope and priority areas
* Summarize the research focus

### Critical prompts

* Which parts of the service shape the experience most?
* Where do teams, systems, or policies create friction for one another?
* What must be in scope for the work to be credible?
* What can be excluded without distorting the problem?

## Sequencing and change pattern

Use for roadmap and business case sessions where the team needs a practical path forward.

### Best for

* Package C roadmap and business case session

### Focus

* What happens now, next, and later
* Key dependencies
* Ownership and operating-model implications
* ROI hypothesis
* Major decision points

### Default structure

* Reconfirm the target-state direction
* Identify dependencies and change implications
* Sequence near-, mid-, and longer-term moves
* Link the plan to business outcomes
* Capture decision points and next steps

### Critical prompts

* What should happen first to reduce delivery risk?
* Which dependencies could block progress?
* What organizational changes are required?
* How should the business case be framed at this stage?
