# Current journey patterns

Use this reference to synthesize the Package B current-state journey.

## Synthesis objective

The purpose of the current-state journey is to create a shared view of how the selected service or product experience works today from both the user and service perspective.

By the end of the synthesis, the team should have enough clarity on:

* How the journey unfolds across stages
* Where users, teams, and systems create friction for one another
* Which problems matter most
* Which opportunity areas and decision criteria should shape the future-state concept

## Recommended synthesis flow

### 1. Reconfirm the journey in scope

Start by restating:

* The selected journey or service area
* The user groups that matter most
* The business case and outcomes the work is meant to influence

### 2. Lay out the end-to-end current journey

Capture:

* The main stages
* The user goals and actions in each stage
* The touchpoints and channels involved

### 3. Add the service and system layer

Capture:

* Which internal teams influence each stage
* Which systems, approvals, or dependencies shape the experience
* Where service logic or operational constraints affect the journey

### 4. Pull out the main breakdowns

Look for:

* Repeated hesitation points
* Handoffs that create delay or rework
* Moments where user need and internal process conflict
* Breakdowns that are material to business outcomes or concept direction

### 5. Distill opportunity areas and decision criteria

Summarize:

* Which parts of the journey need the strongest concept response
* Which user priorities are most important
* Which criteria the later concept work should meet

## What to capture in the journey

At minimum, capture:

* Journey stages
* User goals and actions
* Touchpoints and channels
* Internal teams or systems involved
* Friction points and breakdowns
* Opportunity areas
* Decision criteria for future-state work

## Opportunity-area framing

Opportunity areas should:

* Be grounded in repeated needs or breakdowns
* Link clearly to user value, service quality, or delivery risk
* Be framed as practical areas for concept response rather than abstract ideals

Decision criteria should:

* Reflect what the later concept must do well
* Translate research into useful design and prioritization guidance
* Stay concrete enough to guide later workshop choices

## Common watchouts

* The map becomes too feature-focused and misses service and operational friction
* The journey scope widens beyond what was agreed in the scoping workshop
* The synthesis repeats interview anecdotes instead of pulling out patterns
* Opportunity areas become vague aspirations rather than practical focus areas
* The map is too detailed to support a shared decision conversation
* Evidence gaps are hidden because the team wants a cleaner current-state picture
