# Option review patterns

Use this reference to compare 1-2 solution directions in Package A.

## Contents

* Review objective
* Recommended review flow
* Criteria categories
* Tradeoff prompts
* Common watchouts

## Review objective

The purpose of the option review is to narrow the discovery sprint to one preferred direction using shared criteria rather than internal opinion alone.

By the end of the review, the team should have enough clarity on:

* Which direction is currently strongest
* Why that direction is stronger than the alternative
* Which tradeoffs the choice creates
* Which risks or assumptions still need checking

## Recommended review flow

### 1. Reconfirm the decision and context

Start by restating:

* The decision the sprint must support
* The selected problem area
* The target users and success measures
* The main breakdowns and constraints already identified

### 2. Confirm the options in play

Clarify:

* Which 1-2 directions are genuinely under consideration
* What each direction is trying to improve
* What is materially different between them

### 3. Agree the comparison criteria

Use criteria that cover:

* User value
* Business relevance
* Delivery feasibility
* Risk and dependency exposure

### 4. Compare each direction against the criteria

Ask:

* How well does each direction address the key breakdowns?
* Which users benefit most?
* What new risks or dependencies does each direction create?
* Which direction is more practical to move into delivery?

### 5. Surface the tradeoffs and narrow the choice

Capture:

* What is gained by choosing one path
* What is lost, deferred, or left unresolved
* Which assumptions still matter most

### 6. Playback the preferred direction

Summarize:

* The current preferred direction
* Why it is preferred
* The main risks, dependencies, and checks before recommendation work

## Criteria categories

At minimum, use:

* User impact: how clearly the direction improves the target step for the relevant users or staff
* Business relevance: how closely the direction supports the business question and success measures
* Delivery feasibility: how realistic the direction is given current constraints and likely effort
* Risk exposure: what assumptions, dependencies, or unintended consequences come with the direction

## Tradeoff prompts

Use prompts like:

* Which direction solves the more important problem?
* Which direction creates the least avoidable delivery risk?
* What are we choosing not to do if we pick this path?
* Which assumption matters most if this direction is wrong?
* Which dependency could weaken this direction after the sprint?
* Are we preferring this direction because it is genuinely stronger, or just more familiar?

## Common watchouts

* The review becomes a brainstorm instead of a decision
* The options are too vague to compare properly
* Different participants use different criteria without noticing
* Delivery feasibility is ignored in favor of concept enthusiasm
* The group avoids naming what is lost or deferred by the preferred direction
* A preferred option is declared without recording the risks or checks that still matter
