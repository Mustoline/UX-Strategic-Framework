# Contextual Fieldwork Runner

Use this skill for Package C Activity `2.1`: the fieldwork stream that studies the service in practice rather than relying only on stated process or stakeholder opinion.

This skill should help the team review the best available service and operational evidence, plan and run contextual interviews, observations, or shadowing sessions, and capture the workarounds, delays, ownership gaps, and hidden effort that later current-state blueprinting must make visible.

This skill should build on the helper layer:

* Use [evidence-ingest](../evidence-ingest/SKILL.md) to normalize analytics, support patterns, operational data, and prior service inputs before or during fieldwork
* Use [artifact-writer](../artifact-writer/SKILL.md) to draft session summaries and the fieldwork synthesis cleanly
* Use [ux-strategy-reviewer](../ux-strategy-reviewer/SKILL.md) as the final quality check on client-facing outputs

It should also build on the existing interview skill where useful:

* Use [discovery-interview](../discovery-interview/SKILL.md) as a source of reusable interview structure when contextual interviews are part of the fieldwork, but keep this skill focused on service reality, observation, and operating-model insight rather than product specification discovery

It should use earlier Package C outputs where relevant:

* [executive-and-service-owner-interviewer](../executive-and-service-owner-interviewer/SKILL.md) for the business case, strategic priorities, service pressures, and investment questions
* [ecosystem-workshop-facilitator](../ecosystem-workshop-facilitator/SKILL.md) for the agreed service scope, ecosystem structure, critical service moments, and study focus

## What this skill should produce

For most requests, produce a fieldwork pack with:

* A fieldwork objective tied to the service problem and the decisions ahead
* A fieldwork plan covering 5-8 sessions, participant or role mix, and session type
* A contextual guide for interviews, observation, or shadowing
* A note template focused on behaviors, workarounds, delays, hidden effort, ownership gaps, and breakdowns across channels, teams, and systems
* Short summaries for each fieldwork session
* A first synthesis of repeated patterns the later service blueprint must expose

## Operating modes

### Prep

Before fieldwork begins, confirm:

* Which service, journey, or operational slice is in scope
* Which users, customers, frontline staff, or operational roles need to be observed or interviewed
* Which service moments and breakdown areas the ecosystem work has identified as most important
* What operational data, support patterns, or analytics already exist
* What permissions, observation constraints, or confidentiality limits affect the fieldwork

Then:

* Use `evidence-ingest` to review relevant service and operational evidence before the sessions
* Use the fieldwork flow in [contextual-fieldwork-patterns.md](references/contextual-fieldwork-patterns.md)
* Prepare the guide and note structure in [contextual-fieldwork-templates.md](references/contextual-fieldwork-templates.md)

### Run

During fieldwork:

* Keep the sessions tied to the agreed service scope and critical moments
* Study what participants actually do, not only how they describe the process
* Capture where work moves across channels, teams, or systems and where effort or delay accumulates
* Look for workarounds, rework, duplication, escalation, and ownership gaps
* Distinguish direct observation from interpretation and open questions
* Preserve context that will matter later in the blueprint, such as timing, handoffs, dependencies, and invisible supporting work

### Synthesize

After fieldwork:

* Produce short summaries for each session
* Pull out repeated breakdowns, workarounds, hidden effort, and ownership gaps across the fieldwork
* Show where the service behaves differently in practice than in formal process descriptions
* Distill what the current-state service blueprint should make visible next
* If needed, hand the draft to `artifact-writer` and then to `ux-strategy-reviewer`

## Core research objectives

The contextual fieldwork should answer:

* How does the service actually work in practice across channels, teams, and systems?
* Where do workarounds, delays, duplication, or hidden effort appear?
* Where are ownership gaps, handoff issues, or operational constraints affecting the experience?
* Which service breakdowns matter most to cost, efficiency, risk, or lost value?
* What should the later service blueprint make visible for stakeholders?

## Research rules

* Observe the service in context wherever possible instead of relying only on retrospective description
* Focus on how work moves across people, teams, channels, and systems
* Capture invisible effort, workaround behavior, and ownership gaps, not just visible user friction
* Preserve conflict between formal process and lived reality where it appears
* Treat 5-8 sessions as directional service evidence that exposes patterns, not as exhaustive proof
* Prefer a usable fieldwork synthesis over polished but flattened summaries

## Minimum output standard

Unless the user asks for something else, return these sections:

1. Fieldwork objective
2. Fieldwork plan
3. Contextual guide
4. Note structure
5. Workarounds, delays, hidden effort, and ownership gaps
6. Questions for current-state blueprinting

## Boundaries

This skill covers Activity `C2.1` only. It prepares and runs the fieldwork stream and first synthesis layer for Package C. It does not replace the later current-state service blueprint, target-state concept work, validation, or roadmap work.
