# Contextual fieldwork patterns

Use this reference to structure the Package C fieldwork stream.

## Contents

* Fieldwork objective

* What to capture in each session
* Synthesis framing
* Common watchouts

## Fieldwork objective

The purpose of the fieldwork is to understand how the service works in practice before the team builds the current-state service blueprint.

By the end of the fieldwork, the team should have enough clarity on:

* How work actually moves across channels, teams, and systems
* Where delays, duplication, escalation, or hidden effort appear
* Which workarounds or ownership gaps are keeping the service functioning
* Where the formal process differs from lived reality
* What the later service blueprint needs to make visible

## Recommended fieldwork flow

### 1. Reconfirm the study focus

Start by restating:

* The service or operational slice in scope
* The critical service moments to study
* The strategic themes and investment questions already identified

### 2. Review existing evidence

Pull in:

* Analytics, support patterns, and operational data
* Prior process descriptions or service material
* Known assumptions or tensions from earlier Package C work

### 3. Plan the fieldwork mix

Define:

* Which sessions are contextual interviews
* Which sessions are observation or shadowing
* Which roles, teams, users, or staff groups need to be represented

### 4. Run the sessions in context

Capture:

* What participants do, not only what they say
* What tools, systems, handoffs, or workarounds they rely on
* Where effort, delay, uncertainty, or ownership confusion appears

### 5. Synthesize repeated service breakdowns

Pull out:

* Workarounds that keep the service moving
* Hidden effort that the formal model does not show
* Delays, duplication, and escalation patterns
* Ownership or dependency gaps that matter to the next phase

## What to capture in each session

At minimum, capture:

* Participant role or context
* Session type and observed service moment
* Actions, touchpoints, systems, and supporting work involved
* Workarounds, delays, duplication, or escalation
* Ownership gaps, dependencies, and open questions

## Synthesis framing

The final synthesis should:

* Show where the current service depends on informal effort or workaround behavior
* Highlight where time, cost, support burden, or risk accumulates
* Translate raw field observations into input for the current-state blueprint

Treat the fieldwork as reality testing for the service model, not as a cleaned-up process summary.

## Common watchouts

* Sessions rely too heavily on participant recollection and miss live context
* The team captures anecdotes but not repeated patterns
* Formal process language overrides what was actually observed
* Hidden effort is noticed but not written down clearly
* The fieldwork scope widens beyond the agreed service boundary
* The synthesis becomes descriptive but does not point clearly to blueprint needs
