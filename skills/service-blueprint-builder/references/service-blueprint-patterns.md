# Service blueprint patterns

Use this reference to structure the Package C current-state service blueprint.

## Contents

* Blueprint objective

## Blueprint objective

The purpose of the current-state service blueprint is to make the service model visible so stakeholders can see where the service is leaking value before target-state work begins.

By the end of the blueprinting work, the team should have enough clarity on:

* How the service works across front-stage and back-stage layers
* Where delays, duplication, hidden effort, and ownership gaps sit
* Which systems, processes, or dependencies add friction or cost
* Which parts of the service model matter most to the later target-state work
* What the current service is costing in effort, risk, or lost value

## Recommended blueprint flow

### 1. Reconfirm the service scope and study focus

Start by restating:

* The service or operational slice in scope
* The critical service moments being mapped
* The strategic themes and fieldwork patterns the blueprint should reflect

### 2. Lay out the service stages and interactions

Capture:

* The main stages or moments of the service
* The user or external interactions at each stage
* The front-stage actions visible to the customer or stakeholder

### 3. Add back-stage and support layers

Capture:

* Internal teams or roles involved
* Back-stage actions and supporting processes
* Systems, handoffs, approvals, or dependencies shaping the service

### 4. Mark breakdowns and hotspots

Look for:

* Delay points
* Duplication or repeated effort
* Cost or effort hotspots
* Ownership gaps and escalation patterns

### 5. Distill implications for target-state work

Summarize:

* Which parts of the current model create the most damaging friction
* Which issues matter most to value, efficiency, or service quality
* What the future-state model should change first

## What the blueprint must contain

At minimum, capture:

* Service stages or moments
* User or external interactions
* Front-stage actions
* Back-stage actions
* Supporting processes and systems
* Dependencies, breakdowns, and hotspots

## Hotspot framing

The final output should:

* Show where time, effort, support burden, or coordination cost accumulates
* Make ownership gaps and dependency issues visible rather than implied
* Connect the operational pain to the service problem and value at stake

Treat the blueprint as an evidence-backed operating view, not a tidy ideal-state process map.

## Common watchouts

* The blueprint becomes a clean process diagram and hides what actually goes wrong
* Front-stage activity is shown without the back-stage work that makes it possible
* The map includes too much detail to support a strategic discussion
* Hotspots are listed but not connected to service impact or value loss
* Ownership gaps are softened because they are politically sensitive
* The blueprint is too abstract to guide future-state decisions
