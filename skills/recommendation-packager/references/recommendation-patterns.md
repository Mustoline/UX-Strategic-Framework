# Recommendation patterns

Use this reference to package the final recommendation for Package A.

* Packaging objective
* Recommended packaging flow

## Packaging objective

The purpose of the recommendation package is to turn the discovery sprint into a decision-ready output that gives the sponsor a clearer basis for the next delivery step.

By the end of the packaging work, the team should have enough clarity on:

* What decision the sponsor needs to make
* Which direction is recommended
* Why that direction is the strongest choice
* What should happen now, later, or not yet
* What still needs validation before implementation begins

## Recommended packaging flow

### 1. Reconfirm the decision and preferred direction

Start by restating:

* The decision the sponsor needs to make
* The preferred direction from the option review
* The target users, success measures, and constraints shaping the decision

### 2. Pull together the core rationale

Summarize:

* The strongest evidence behind the recommendation
* The key breakdowns the direction responds to
* The tradeoffs accepted by choosing this path

### 3. Define the scope of the recommendation

Clarify:

* What should be built or pursued next
* What should be deferred
* What is explicitly not recommended yet

### 4. Make the direction tangible

Create:

* A simple concept brief or lightweight prototype brief
* The key screens, steps, or moments the concept needs to illustrate
* The questions the concept should help stakeholders review

### 5. Capture risks, dependencies, and validation needs

List:

* What still rests on assumption
* What dependencies could block progress
* What checks should happen before implementation starts

### 6. End with a clear next step

State:

* What decision should be made now
* What the client should do immediately next
* What the next delivery or validation move should be

## What the document must contain

At minimum, the recommendation document should include:

* The decision to support
* The recommended direction
* The rationale behind the choice
* The relevant target users and success measures
* What to build now and what to defer
* Risks, assumptions, dependencies, and next steps

## What the concept brief must contain

At minimum, the concept brief should include:

* The purpose of the concept
* The users or staff groups it is for
* The key moments or screens it needs to illustrate
* What the concept is meant to test, clarify, or make tangible
* What is explicitly out of scope for the concept

## Common watchouts

* The recommendation repeats findings without making a decision clear
* The scope is too broad for a near-term delivery step
* The concept brief is treated like a full specification
* Risks and assumptions are hidden because the team wants a cleaner conclusion
* The document does not clearly separate build now, defer, and validate
* The next step is implied rather than stated directly
