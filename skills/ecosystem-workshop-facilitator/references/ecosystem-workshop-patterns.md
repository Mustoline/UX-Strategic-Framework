# Ecosystem workshop patterns

Use this reference to structure the Package C ecosystem workshop.

## Workshop objective

The purpose of the ecosystem workshop is to align stakeholders on the service scope before the team studies the service in practice.

By the end of the workshop, the team should have enough clarity on:

* Which departments, touchpoints, systems, and operational actors shape the service
* Where the service boundary should sit for this phase of work
* Which service moments matter most to examine in fieldwork
* Where major handoffs, dependencies, or ownership questions are likely to sit
* What the later blueprinting work needs to make visible

## Recommended workshop flow

### 1. Reconfirm the strategic frame

Start by restating:

* The service or initiative in scope
* Why the work matters now
* Which strategic themes, risks, and investment questions came out of the interviews

### 2. Map the service ecosystem

Capture:

* The main departments and teams shaping the service
* The key customer or user touchpoints
* The important systems, channels, and operational actors involved

### 3. Define the service boundary

Clarify:

* What is in scope for this phase of work
* What is out of scope but still relevant context
* Which cross-functional dependencies need to stay visible

### 4. Identify the critical service moments

Look for:

* Moments where value is created, lost, delayed, or made expensive
* Moments where handoffs, approvals, or ownership gaps affect the experience
* Moments the fieldwork and blueprint should follow most closely

### 5. Close with study focus and risks

Summarize:

* Which parts of the ecosystem the later study should prioritize
* Which unresolved scope questions remain
* Which risks or blind spots need attention in the next phase

## What the ecosystem map must contain

At minimum, capture:

* Departments or teams
* Touchpoints and channels
* Systems and operational actors
* Key handoffs and dependencies
* The agreed service boundary
* Critical service moments to study next

## Study-focus framing

The final output should:

* Show enough of the ecosystem to guide later fieldwork without expanding into the whole organization
* Name where the team expects the most important service friction or lost value to sit
* Translate the strategic interview themes into a practical study focus

Treat the ecosystem map as a working scope tool, not a perfect enterprise architecture view.

## Common watchouts

* The workshop becomes a broad organization chart exercise rather than a service-scope decision
* The conversation stays inside one department's perspective
* The ecosystem map becomes too abstract to guide fieldwork
* Participants avoid naming ownership gaps or sensitive handoffs
* The scope grows so wide that the next phase becomes unmanageable
* Critical service moments are implied but not named clearly
