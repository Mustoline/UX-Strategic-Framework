# Source patterns

Use this reference to review different evidence types without flattening them into one generic summary.

## Contents

* Analytics
* Support issues
* Sales and commercial input
* Prior research
* Operational data
* Interview and observation notes
* Cross-source synthesis

## Analytics

Use analytics to understand patterns at scale, not motives on their own.

### Best for

* Drop-off points
* Conversion friction
* Usage concentration
* Time-on-task patterns
* Device, channel, or segment differences

### Questions to ask

* Where do people stop, hesitate, or abandon?
* Which flows or pages concentrate the most friction?
* Are there meaningful differences by channel, segment, or device?
* Is the problem stable over time or tied to a recent change?

### Watchouts

* Analytics can show behavior but not always the reason behind it
* Weak instrumentation can create false confidence
* A large number is not automatically a strategically important problem

## Support issues

Use support material to understand recurring pain points, language used by customers or staff, and the operational burden behind the experience.

### Best for

* Repeat complaints
* Confusing steps or messages
* Escalation triggers
* Internal effort caused by service failure

### Questions to ask

* Which issues repeat most often?
* Which issues create the most effort or delay?
* Are there issues that indicate a broken step in the journey rather than isolated confusion?
* What language do customers or staff use to describe the problem?

### Watchouts

* Support volume does not always equal strategic importance
* Some important problems are underreported because users drop out silently
* Support notes often mix symptom and cause

## Sales and commercial input

Use sales input to understand objections, lost momentum, and commercial consequences tied to the experience.

### Best for

* Objections that block conversion or adoption
* Questions that slow the buying process
* Misalignment between promise and experience
* Commercial signals about value or trust

### Questions to ask

* What objections recur most often?
* Where does the experience create hesitation or loss of confidence?
* Which issues affect conversion, retention, or deal quality?
* Which complaints are really about service design, not messaging alone?

### Watchouts

* Sales input is often interpretive rather than direct customer evidence
* Strong opinions from a few people can skew the picture
* Commercial teams may frame symptoms as messaging problems when the issue sits deeper in the service

## Prior research

Use prior research to avoid rediscovering what is already known and to see whether earlier findings still matter.

### Best for

* Existing user needs and barriers
* Previously identified breakdowns
* Decision criteria
* Earlier concept reactions

### Questions to ask

* Which findings are still relevant to the current decision?
* What patterns repeat across older and newer evidence?
* What was learned previously but never acted on?
* Where is the research no longer current enough to rely on?

### Watchouts

* Older research may no longer fit the current context
* Prior recommendations may be stronger than the evidence behind them
* Teams often remember conclusions but forget the limits of the sample

## Operational data

Use operational data to understand service load, handoff friction, internal waste, and ownership issues.

### Best for

* Delay and cycle-time patterns
* Rework or duplication
* Escalation rates
* Manual workarounds
* Cost-to-serve signals

### Questions to ask

* Where does internal effort spike?
* Which steps depend on manual workarounds?
* Where do teams wait on one another or re-handle the same case?
* Which patterns suggest service-model or ownership problems?

### Watchouts

* Operational data is often fragmented across teams and systems
* Efficiency signals can hide quality or experience tradeoffs
* Internal metrics may reflect policy choices rather than user need

## Interview and observation notes

Use interviews and observations to understand intent, barriers, decision criteria, and lived experience.

### Best for

* Motivations and expectations
* Decision points
* Emotional or practical friction
* Workarounds
* Context the systems do not show

### Questions to ask

* What are people trying to get done?
* Where do they lose trust, clarity, or momentum?
* What workarounds do they use today?
* Which quotes point to a broader recurring pattern?

### Watchouts

* Small samples give depth, not scale
* Individual stories need careful synthesis before they become general findings
* Interviews can overrepresent stated preference if not checked against behavior or operational evidence

## Cross-source synthesis

After reviewing the sources separately, combine them carefully.

### Look for

* Patterns repeated across more than one source
* Issues that show both user pain and business impact
* Conflicts between what teams believe and what evidence shows
* Areas where evidence is thin and should not yet drive a strong recommendation

### Useful framing

* Strong signal: repeated across multiple credible sources
* Directional signal: useful, but still needs validation
* Assumption: currently a belief, not supported by enough evidence
* Conflict: sources point in different directions and should be explored further
