# Map patterns

Use this reference to choose the right map structure for the activity and decision type.

## Contents

* Journey-step map
* Current-state journey map
* Ecosystem map
* Service blueprint
* Future-state journey or service map

## Journey-step map

Use when the team needs to understand one bounded slice of the experience in enough detail to support a near-term decision.

### Best for

* Package A journey-step mapping
* Narrow workflow or feature-area analysis

### Focus

* One selected step or slice of the journey
* The actors involved at that step
* Immediate touchpoints and dependencies
* Where time, confidence, or momentum is lost

### Core sections

* Step or moment in scope
* User or staff actions
* Touchpoints and systems involved
* Pain points or friction
* Internal implications

### Best question it should answer

* Where does this specific part of the experience break down today?

## Current-state journey map

Use when the team needs an end-to-end view of how the experience works today across multiple steps and actors.

### Best for

* Package B current-state journey work
* Current-state service understanding before concept definition

### Focus

* Sequence across the full journey in scope
* User goals and actions
* Internal touchpoints and support moments
* Decision points and failure points
* Experience and service breakdowns

### Core sections

* Journey stages
* User goals and actions
* Touchpoints and channels
* Internal teams or systems involved
* Pain points, delays, and opportunity areas

### Best question it should answer

* Where across the end-to-end journey is value won or lost today?

## Ecosystem map

Use when the team needs to define the broader service landscape and agree scope across departments, touchpoints, systems, and actors.

### Best for

* Package C ecosystem workshop
* Cross-functional service-scoping work

### Focus

* Departments and actors
* Touchpoints and channels
* Systems and platforms
* External and internal dependencies
* Service boundaries and priority areas

### Core sections

* Actors and roles
* Touchpoints and channels
* Systems and data dependencies
* Operational relationships
* In-scope versus out-of-scope areas

### Best question it should answer

* What parts of the service landscape shape the experience enough to be in scope?

## Service blueprint

Use when the team needs to expose the service logic behind the experience, especially handoffs, ownership gaps, duplication, delay, and hidden effort.

### Best for

* Package C current-state blueprint
* Complex cross-team or cross-system service analysis

### Focus

* Front-stage and back-stage activity
* Processes, systems, and ownership
* Dependencies and handoffs
* Delay, duplication, and cost signals

### Core sections

* User actions or customer steps
* Front-stage interactions
* Back-stage processes
* Supporting systems and data
* Ownership, dependencies, and operational pain points

### Best question it should answer

* Where does the service model itself create friction, waste, or risk?

## Future-state journey or service map

Use when the team needs to define how the future experience or service should work rather than document the current state.

### Best for

* Package B future-state concept work
* Package C future-state service-model work

### Focus

* Intended future flow
* Experience principles
* Service changes needed behind the scenes
* Dependencies and feasibility signals

### Core sections

* Future stages or flow
* Intended user and service outcomes
* Supporting changes in process, content, ownership, or data
* Risks, assumptions, and validation points

### Best question it should answer

* What should the future experience or service do differently, and what must change to support it?
