# User research patterns

Use this reference to plan and run the Package B interview stream.

## Contents

* Research objective

* Interview sections and prompts
* Synthesis focus

## Research objective

The purpose of the interview stream is to give the engagement a grounded view of user needs, barriers, and decision points in the selected journey or service area.

By the end of the interviews, the team should have enough clarity on:

* What users are trying to get done
* Where they hesitate, struggle, or abandon
* Which barriers matter most
* Which criteria shape their choices and confidence
* Which patterns the later journey work must take seriously

## Recommended interview flow

### 1. Reconfirm the research frame

Start by restating:

* The journey or service area in scope
* The user groups to recruit
* The decisions the research needs to support
* The main assumptions or evidence gaps from the scoping workshop

### 2. Review existing evidence before interviews

Check:

* What analytics, feedback, support issues, or prior research already suggest
* Which signals are strong enough to sharpen the guide
* Which assumptions still need direct user input

### 3. Run the interview around the lived journey

Move through:

* Context and recent experience
* User goal and trigger
* What they tried to do
* Where they hesitated or got blocked
* What influenced their decision
* What they did instead, if the journey failed

### 4. Capture pattern-level notes after each interview

Record:

* Main need
* Main barrier
* Main decision point
* Main workaround or support need

### 5. Pull out cross-interview patterns

Look for:

* Repeated needs
* Repeated friction points
* Decision criteria that appear across multiple participants
* Conflicts between stated preference and observed reality

## Interview sections and prompts

### Context and trigger

* What were you trying to do?
* What started the process?
* Why did you need to do this now?

### Journey and behavior

* Walk me through what happened step by step
* What did you expect at that point?
* Where did you pause, re-read, or hesitate?
* What felt unclear or risky?

### Barriers and workarounds

* What made this difficult?
* Did you leave the flow, ask someone, or look elsewhere for help?
* What did you do when you were unsure?

### Decision points

* What mattered most when deciding what to do next?
* What information did you need but not get?
* What would have increased your confidence?

## Synthesis focus

At minimum, pull out:

* Top user needs
* Top barriers
* Top decision criteria
* Repeated hesitation points
* Main opportunity areas for current-journey mapping

## Common watchouts

* The guide becomes a feature wishlist instead of a journey interview
* Participants are recruited too broadly to inform the selected scope
* Interviewers lead participants toward the team's existing assumptions
* Notes capture quotes but not patterns
* The synthesis treats 4-6 interviews like statistical proof
* The team jumps into future solutions before the current experience is understood
