# User Research Runner

Use this skill for Package B Activity `1.2`: the interview stream that gives the engagement a usable evidence base on user needs, barriers, and decision criteria before current-journey and concept work begin.

This skill should help the team review the best available evidence, recruit the right users, prepare a focused interview guide, run or support 4-6 semi-structured interviews, and produce a first synthesis that later Package B skills can build on.

This skill should build on the helper layer:

* Use [evidence-ingest](../evidence-ingest/SKILL.md) to normalize existing evidence before interviews begin
* Use [artifact-writer](../artifact-writer/SKILL.md) to draft interview summaries and the concise insight summary
* Use [ux-strategy-reviewer](../ux-strategy-reviewer/SKILL.md) as the final quality check on client-facing outputs

It should also build on the existing interview skill where useful:

* Use [discovery-interview](../discovery-interview/SKILL.md) as a source of reusable interview thinking, but keep this skill focused on strategic UX and service-design research rather than product specification discovery

It should use earlier Package B outputs where relevant:

* Use [scoping-workshop-facilitator](../scoping-workshop-facilitator/SKILL.md) for the journey in scope, business case, success measures, and discovery questions

## What this skill should produce

For most requests, produce a research pack with:

* A research objective tied to the journey in scope and the decisions ahead
* A participant profile or recruitment brief
* A semi-structured interview guide
* A note template for consistent capture across interviews
* Interview summaries and a first synthesis of needs, barriers, and decision points

## Operating modes

### Prep

Before the interviews, confirm:

* Which journey, product, or service area is in scope
* Which user groups need to be represented
* Which decisions the research must inform
* What evidence already exists
* How many interviews can realistically be run and in what format

Then:

* Use `evidence-ingest` to review analytics, customer feedback, support issues, sales input, or prior research before the interviews
* Use the interview flow in [user-research-patterns.md](references/user-research-patterns.md)
* Prepare the interview guide and note structure in [user-research-templates.md](references/user-research-templates.md)

### Run

During the interviews:

* Keep the conversation tied to the selected journey and the user's real goals
* Ask open questions that reveal needs, barriers, decision points, and workarounds
* Push beyond first answers when the participant stays vague or describes a preference without context
* Capture concrete moments of hesitation, confusion, and comparison logic
* Separate what the participant says, what the team infers, and what still needs further validation

### Synthesize

After the interviews:

* Produce short summaries for each session
* Pull out patterns across interviews rather than repeating transcripts
* Distill the top user needs, barriers, and decision criteria
* Show what the current-journey work should examine next
* If needed, hand the draft to `artifact-writer` and then to `ux-strategy-reviewer`

## Core research objectives

The interview stream should answer:

* What are users trying to achieve in the selected journey or service area?
* Where do they hesitate, lose confidence, or drop momentum?
* What barriers or workarounds shape the current experience?
* Which criteria shape their decisions?
* Which patterns matter most for the later current-journey and concept work?

## Output templates

Use [user-research-templates.md](references/user-research-templates.md) for:

* Research objective
* Recruitment brief
* Interview guide
* Interview note template
* Interview summary
* Insight summary

Adapt the output to the journey and decision in scope rather than filling every section mechanically.

## Research rules

* Recruit for relevance to the journey in scope, not generic demographic spread
* Use the existing evidence to sharpen the guide, not to confirm assumptions prematurely
* Focus on lived behavior, decision moments, barriers, and workarounds
* Prefer pattern-level insight over polished but unsupported conclusions
* If interview evidence conflicts with existing assumptions, preserve the conflict clearly
* Treat 4-6 interviews as directional strategic evidence, not statistical proof

## Minimum output standard

Unless the user asks for something else, return these sections:

1. Research objective
2. Participant profile or recruitment brief
3. Interview guide
4. Note structure
5. Top user needs, barriers, and decision points
6. Questions for later journey and concept work

## Boundaries

This skill covers Activity `B1.2` only. It prepares and runs the user interview stream and first synthesis layer. It does not replace the later current-journey synthesis, future-state concept work, or prioritization work.
