# Sponsor workshop patterns

Use this reference to run a focused 90-minute sponsor workshop for Package A.

## Session objective

The purpose of the sponsor workshop is to create a shared decision frame for the discovery sprint.

By the end of the session, the team should have enough alignment on:

* The business question the sprint must support
* The users or staff groups that matter most
* The success measures that will signal progress
* The main business or technical constraints
* The questions the sprint still needs to answer

## Recommended 90-minute flow

### 1. Opening and objective framing: 10 minutes

Focus:

* Confirm why this sprint is happening now
* Confirm the decision the client needs to make
* Confirm what this workshop is and is not for

### 2. Problem framing and business context: 20 minutes

Focus:

* Clarify the business trigger
* Clarify the current pain point or opportunity
* Clarify the delivery risk of moving ahead without enough clarity

### 3. Target users and journey focus: 20 minutes

Focus:

* Identify who is most affected
* Narrow the journey step, workflow, or feature area in scope
* Identify where experience or service problems seem most acute

### 4. Success measures and constraints: 20 minutes

Focus:

* Define what better would look like
* Translate goals into concrete measures or signals
* Identify the fixed constraints and likely dependencies

### 5. Open questions and sprint priorities: 15 minutes

Focus:

* Name what is still assumption
* Identify what the sprint must clarify first
* Note where evidence is missing or conflicting

### 6. Playback and close: 5 minutes

Focus:

* Play back the agreed decision frame
* Confirm the most important open questions
* Confirm immediate next steps

## Critical questions by section

### Problem framing and business context

* What problem are we actually trying to solve?
* Why does it matter now?
* What happens if the organization does nothing?
* What decision must be easier after this sprint?

### Target users and journey focus

* Which users, customers, or staff groups are most affected?
* Which part of the journey or workflow matters most right now?
* Where do people seem to lose time, confidence, or momentum today?
* Which internal teams or systems shape that experience most?

### Success measures

* What would count as a useful outcome from this work?
* Which business, service, or operational signals would tell us the direction is better?
* What would make this sprint feel successful to the sponsor?

### Constraints

* Which business constraints are fixed?
* Which technical or operational constraints are already known?
* Which constraints are real, and which are assumptions we have not tested yet?

### Open questions and sprint priorities

* What do we know already?
* What do we still assume?
* Which unanswered question is most important before delivery starts?
* What should the sprint explicitly not try to solve?

## Follow-up probes

Use these when sponsor answers stay too broad:

* Can you make that more specific?
* Which user group are you referring to exactly?
* How would we recognize improvement in practice?
* Is that a fixed constraint, or is it simply the current expectation?
* What decision would be different if we learned the answer to that question?
* What is the risk if we move ahead without clarifying this?

## Common watchouts

* The workshop turns into a broad strategy conversation instead of a bounded sprint decision
* Sponsors speak about solutions before the problem and scope are clear
* The group names generic users rather than the most affected users
* Success measures stay abstract and cannot guide later prioritization
* Constraints are treated as fixed even when they are only assumptions
* Open questions are lost because the session ends without playback
