# Executive and service-owner interview patterns

Use this reference to structure the Package C executive and service-owner interview stream.

## Contents

* Interview objective

## Interview objective

The purpose of these interviews is to create a strategic framing layer for the service-redesign work before the team maps the ecosystem or studies the service in practice.

By the end of the interview stream, the team should have enough clarity on:

* Where the current service model is under pressure
* Which outcomes and risks matter most to executives and service owners
* Which investment questions the wider work must help answer
* Where there are cross-functional tensions, dependencies, or ownership gaps
* What the next Package C steps should examine in more detail

## Recommended interview flow

### 1. Reconfirm the service and decision frame

Start by restating:

* The service, proposition, or initiative in scope
* Why the work is being considered now
* Which decisions or investments the broader Package C work should support

### 2. Clarify the business case and strategic priorities

Capture:

* Which business, service, or operating-model outcomes matter most
* What strategic priorities or pressures make the initiative important now
* Which external or internal changes are increasing pressure on the current model

### 3. Surface service pressures and operating pain

Ask about:

* Delays, duplication, cost, support burden, or missed-value patterns
* Breakdowns across departments, systems, channels, or handoffs
* Where ownership is unclear or incentives are misaligned

### 4. Clarify risks and tradeoffs

Explore:

* What risks matter if nothing changes
* What risks matter if the wrong change is prioritized
* Which tradeoffs or tensions senior stakeholders already see

### 5. Define the investment questions

Summarize:

* Which strategic questions still need evidence
* Which areas need clearer sequencing, ownership, or ROI logic
* What the later ecosystem, fieldwork, and roadmap steps must make easier to decide

## What to capture in each interview

At minimum, capture:

* Stakeholder role and lens on the service
* Strategic priorities
* Business case logic
* Main service pressures or failure patterns
* Risks, tradeoffs, and constraints
* Investment questions or decision needs

## Strategic-theme framing

The synthesis should pull out:

* Repeated service pressures that cut across interviews
* The value at stake if the current model does not improve
* The main risks or failure modes senior stakeholders want addressed
* The most important investment questions the rest of Package C should answer

Treat this as strategic framing, not a consensus exercise.

## Common watchouts

* Interviews stay too high level and never reach concrete business or service consequences
* The conversation collapses into one department's view of the problem
* Stakeholder politics are mistaken for evidence
* Risks are framed only as delivery risk and not as service or business risk
* The synthesis hides disagreement to create a cleaner story
* Investment questions remain too broad to guide the later Package C work
