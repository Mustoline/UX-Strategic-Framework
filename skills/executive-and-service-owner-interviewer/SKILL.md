# Executive And Service-Owner Interviewer

Use this skill for Package C Activity `1.1`: the executive and service-owner interview stream that creates the strategic framing for the broader service-redesign work.

This skill should help the team understand where the current service model is under pressure, which outcomes and risks matter most to senior stakeholders, and which investment questions the work must support before ecosystem mapping, fieldwork, and roadmap work begin.

This skill should build on the helper layer:

* Use [evidence-ingest](../evidence-ingest/SKILL.md) to normalize existing service, operational, support, or analytics inputs before interviews begin
* Use [artifact-writer](../artifact-writer/SKILL.md) to draft interview summaries, the strategic-theme synthesis, and the executive brief cleanly
* Use [ux-strategy-reviewer](../ux-strategy-reviewer/SKILL.md) as the final quality check on client-facing outputs

It should also build on the existing interview skill where useful:

* Use [discovery-interview](../discovery-interview/SKILL.md) as a source of reusable interview structure, but keep this skill focused on strategic service questions rather than product or specification discovery

## What this skill should produce

For most requests, produce a strategic interview pack with:

* An interview objective tied to the service problem and the decisions ahead
* A stakeholder interview plan covering executives and service owners
* A semi-structured interview guide for 45-60 minute conversations
* A note template focused on business case, strategic priorities, service pressures, risks, and investment questions
* Short summaries for each interview
* A synthesis of strategic themes, main risks, and the investment questions that should guide the rest of Package C

## Operating modes

### Prep

Before the interviews, confirm:

* Which service, proposition, or cross-functional initiative is in scope
* Which executive and service-owner roles need to be represented
* Which decisions, investments, or change questions the work must inform
* What evidence or prior strategy material already exists
* How many interviews can realistically be run and in what format

Then:

* Use `evidence-ingest` to review relevant service, operational, support, analytics, or strategy inputs before the interviews
* Use the interview flow in [executive-and-service-owner-patterns.md](references/executive-and-service-owner-patterns.md)
* Prepare the guide and note structure in [executive-and-service-owner-templates.md](references/executive-and-service-owner-templates.md)

### Run

During the interviews:

* Keep the conversation tied to the service model, strategic priorities, and the decisions this work must support
* Ask for concrete pressure points, business consequences, and cross-functional constraints rather than abstract aspiration
* Surface where leaders see risk, cost, duplication, delay, or missed value in the current model
* Distinguish observations, assumptions, politics, and unknowns clearly
* Capture where stakeholder views align and where they conflict
* Separate what is a strategic priority now from what is only a longer-term ambition

### Synthesize

After the interviews:

* Produce short summaries for each conversation
* Distill the top strategic themes, service pressures, risks, and investment questions across the interviews
* Show what the ecosystem workshop and later fieldwork should examine next
* Turn the synthesis into an executive brief if needed
* If needed, hand the draft to `artifact-writer` and then to `ux-strategy-reviewer`

## Core research objectives

The executive and service-owner interviews should answer:

* Where is the current service model under the most pressure?
* Which outcomes, risks, or missed-value areas matter most to senior stakeholders?
* Which strategic priorities should shape the rest of the redesign work?
* Which investment questions or tradeoffs need clearer evidence before a roadmap can be agreed?
* Which tensions across departments, channels, systems, or ownership need the next Package C steps to unpack?

## Research rules

* Interview for strategic relevance and service accountability, not hierarchy alone
* Focus on business consequences, service pressures, ownership issues, and investment logic
* Push beyond stakeholder slogans to concrete examples of delay, cost, duplication, risk, or lost value
* Preserve disagreement where stakeholder perspectives conflict rather than smoothing them into false alignment
* Treat the interviews as a framing layer for the wider Package C work, not as proof in themselves
* Prefer a usable strategic brief over a polished but vague stakeholder summary

## Minimum output standard

Unless the user asks for something else, return these sections:

1. Interview objective
2. Stakeholder interview plan
3. Interview guide
4. Note structure
5. Strategic themes, risks, and investment questions
6. Questions for ecosystem and fieldwork phases

## Boundaries

This skill covers Activity `C1.1` only. It prepares and runs the strategic interview stream and first synthesis layer for Package C. It does not replace the later ecosystem workshop, fieldwork, service blueprinting, validation, or roadmap work.
