# Journey-step patterns

Use this reference to map one selected journey step for Package A.

## Mapping objective

The purpose of the journey-step map is to make one bounded part of the current experience visible enough to guide the next discovery decisions.

By the end of the mapping work, the team should have enough clarity on:

* What happens in the selected step today
* Which actors, touchpoints, and dependencies shape the experience
* Where time, confidence, or momentum is lost
* Which breakdowns matter most to the next delivery decision

## Recommended mapping flow

### 1. Reconfirm the step in scope

Start by restating:

* The selected journey step, workflow slice, or feature-area moment
* The target users or staff groups involved
* The decision this map needs to support

### 2. Map the current sequence

Capture:

* What happens first, next, and last in this step
* Which actors are involved
* Which touchpoints or systems shape the experience

### 3. Identify friction and dependencies

Look for:

* Moments of hesitation, confusion, or delay
* Internal handoffs or approvals that slow progress
* System, data, or process dependencies that create extra effort

### 4. Distinguish the biggest breakdowns

Narrow to:

* The 3-5 breakdowns that have the clearest effect on the user, service, or delivery decision
* The issues that show up repeatedly in evidence or discussion

### 5. Capture implications for the sprint

Summarize:

* Why these breakdowns matter now
* Which parts of the problem still need validation
* What the later option review should keep in view

## What to capture

At minimum, capture:

* Actors involved
* User or staff actions
* Touchpoints or channels
* Systems or approvals
* Friction points
* Dependencies
* Operational or internal implications

## Breakdown prompts

Use prompts like:

* Where does the user or staff member lose time here?
* Where does confidence drop?
* Which handoff creates delay or uncertainty?
* Which dependency adds hidden effort?
* Which issue is most damaging to momentum or delivery confidence?
* Which problem is clearly visible versus still assumed?

## Common watchouts

* The team tries to map the whole journey instead of the selected step
* The map reflects the intended process rather than the real one
* Touchpoints are shown without the dependencies behind them
* Every pain point is treated as equally important
* Internal effort is missed because the focus stays only on the visible interface
* The output becomes too detailed to support a clear next decision
