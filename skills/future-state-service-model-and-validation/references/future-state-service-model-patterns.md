# Future-state service-model patterns

Use this reference to structure the Package C target-state service-model and validation work.

## Objective

The purpose of this step is to define a future-state service direction that responds to the current-state blueprint and test whether it stands up with the people who will use, deliver, or support it.

By the end of the work, the team should have enough clarity on:

* How the future service should work across channels, teams, and systems
* Which parts of the current model need the strongest change
* Whether the proposed direction is understandable, workable, and credible
* Which feedback requires refinement of the model
* Which feasibility signals and dependencies matter to the final roadmap step

## Recommended flow

### 1. Reconfirm the current-state problem and target frame

Start by restating:

* The service scope in focus
* The most important blueprint hotspots and failure patterns
* The strategic priorities and investment questions the future-state model must support

### 2. Define the target-state service model

Capture:

* The future service stages or key moments
* How channels, teams, systems, and ownership should work together
* What should be simpler, faster, clearer, or more reliable in the future state

### 3. Identify the main service shifts

Clarify:

* Which changes in operating model, ownership, process, or service logic are required
* Which assumptions or dependencies the target-state model rests on

### 4. Prepare and run validation

Test with:

* Users, customers, or internal stakeholders who can expose practical friction
* Prompts that reveal understanding, confidence, feasibility concerns, and missing pieces

### 5. Synthesize feedback and refine the model

Pull out:

* Where the model held up
* Where friction, confusion, or feasibility concerns remained
* What should change in the model before the roadmap step

## What the target-state model must contain

At minimum, capture:

* Target-state service stages or moments
* The intended experience and service behavior
* Roles, teams, or systems involved
* Required service or operating-model changes
* Dependencies, assumptions, or constraints that still matter

## Validation framing

The validation output should:

* Show whether the proposed model is understandable and workable
* Capture both positive signals and remaining friction
* Separate refinement needs from bigger unresolved feasibility questions

Treat validation as a stress test for the future-state model, not as a box-ticking exercise.

## Common watchouts

* The target-state model is too abstract to be reviewed meaningfully
* Validation participants are too narrow to expose the real risks
* Feedback is summarized politely and loses the friction signals
* Feasibility concerns are deferred without being documented
* The refined model becomes broader or vaguer instead of clearer
* The output does not leave the roadmap step with usable sequencing signals
