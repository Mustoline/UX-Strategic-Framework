# Package B execution plan: Service concept definition

## Overview

**Timeline:** 2 weeks
**Focus:** Define the right concept for a product or journey before scope and priorities are locked in delivery
**Best used when:** The client knows the opportunity area, but still needs evidence on user needs, the future-state journey, and the supporting service changes

## Core activities

### Phase 1: Framing and recruitment (Days 1-2)

* **Goal:** Set the scope and prepare the research so the work answers the right commercial, user, and delivery questions
* **Activity 1.1: Run a 2-hour scoping workshop with the sponsor and key stakeholders to agree the journey in scope, business case, and success criteria**
    * Clarify the business case, the key decisions ahead, the journey in scope, and the outcomes the work should influence
    * Agree success measures, scope boundaries, and the questions the discovery work must answer
* **Activity 1.2: Conduct 4-6 semi-structured user interviews, typically 45-60 minutes each, run remotely or in person, supported by a review of analytics, customer feedback, support issues, and sales input**
    * Review the best available evidence before interviews begin so the team starts from known signals rather than assumptions
    * Recruit the right users and prepare a focused interview guide around needs, barriers, and decision points
    * Use the interviews to understand what users are trying to achieve, where they hesitate, and what gets in their way

### Phase 2: Research and journey mapping (Days 3-5)

* **Goal:** Understand the current experience from both the user and the service perspective
* **Activity 2.1: Map the current journey to show where the experience and service break down across users, teams, and systems**
    * Map the end-to-end journey around the selected service or product area
    * Make visible where users, internal teams, and systems create friction for one another
    * Distill the research into opportunity areas, user priorities, and decision criteria
    * Align the team around the most important findings

### Phase 3: Concept definition and supporting service changes (Days 6-8)

* **Goal:** Translate insight into a future-state direction that can support delivery planning
* **Activity 3.1: Run a 2-3 hour concept working session to define the future-state journey and the required changes in process, content, ownership, and data**
    * Define the future-state journey and the main experience principles
    * Identify the changes needed in process, content, ownership, or data to support the concept and clarify what delivery depends on beyond screens and functionality

### Phase 4: Prototype, prioritization, and recommendation (Days 9-10)

* **Goal:** Make the concept tangible and turn it into a practical delivery recommendation
* **Activity 4.1: Build a clickable prototype of the core flow and run a 2-hour prioritization workshop to decide what should be delivered first**
    * Create a clickable concept prototype for the core journey and use it to review the proposed direction with stakeholders
    * Rank opportunities based on user value, business relevance, and implementation effort so the client can see what to build first, what to defer, and where more validation is needed

## Client involvement

* **Commitment:** Approximately 1.5-2 days
* **Key action:** Participation in the framing session, journey reviews, and prioritization workshop
* **Input needed:** Access to users or customer-facing teams, existing evidence, and timely review of the target-state concept

## Concrete outcomes

1. **A concise insight summary from 4-6 interviews showing the top user needs, barriers, and decision criteria:** This gives the client a usable evidence base for the decisions ahead, not just raw research notes.
2. **A current-state and future-state journey map showing how the experience should change end to end:** This creates a shared view of what is broken today and what the future journey needs to do differently.
3. **A list of required service changes across process, content, ownership, and data, with the main dependencies called out:** This shows what has to change behind the scenes for the concept to work in practice.
4. **A clickable prototype of the core flow illustrating the proposed experience at the key decision points:** This makes the future-state direction tangible enough for stakeholder review and delivery planning.
5. **A prioritized delivery recommendation showing what to build first, what to defer, and what to validate next:** This turns the discovery work into a practical roadmap for the next phase.
