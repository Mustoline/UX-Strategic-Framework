# Package A execution plan: Discovery sprint

## Overview

**Timeline:** 3-5 business days
**Focus:** Make one near-term delivery decision with better evidence and less internal debate
**Best used when:** The client needs to decide whether and how to improve one specific feature area, workflow, or journey step before development begins

## Core activities

### Phase 1: Decision and evidence baseline (Days 1-2)

* **Goal:** Build a fast, shared understanding of the problem and the decision that needs to be made
* **Activity 1.1: Run a 90-minute sponsor workshop with 3-5 decision-makers to define the business question, target users, success criteria, and constraints**
    * Use the workshop to agree what decision needs to be made before delivery starts
    * Document the target users, the success measures, and the main business or technical constraints
* **Activity 1.2: Review existing evidence from analytics, support issues, sales input, and prior research relevant to the selected problem**
    * Review the strongest signals the client already has, including behavior data, top support issues, sales objections, and previous findings
    * Separate what is already known from what is still assumption so the team does not debate the wrong questions
* **Activity 1.3: Run a 60-90 minute mapping session of the selected journey step to show where users or staff lose time, confidence, or momentum**
    * Map the people, touchpoints, and dependencies shaping the selected problem area
    * Highlight where users or staff lose time, confidence, or momentum today

### Phase 2: Direction comparison (Day 3)

* **Goal:** Compare solution directions using shared criteria and narrow the recommendation to one preferred direction
* **Activity 2.1: Run a 2-hour option review to compare 1-2 solution directions against agreed business, user, and delivery criteria**
    * Compare 1-2 solution directions for the selected problem using the agreed user, business, and delivery criteria
    * Identify which direction is most likely to improve the journey without creating avoidable delivery risk

### Phase 3: Concept development and recommendation (Days 4-5)

* **Goal:** Turn the discovery work into a practical direction that can be used in delivery planning
* **Activity 3.1: Prepare a short recommendation document with a simple concept view or clickable prototype showing what to build next, what to defer, and what still needs validation**
    * Turn the chosen direction into a simple concept view or a 1-2 screen clickable prototype so the recommendation is tangible
    * Summarize what should be built next, what should wait, and which risks, assumptions, or dependencies still need to be resolved

## Client involvement

* **Commitment:** Approximately 4-6 hours
* **Key action:** Participation from the sponsor and relevant stakeholders in the alignment workshop
* **Input needed:** Access to existing evidence, internal subject matter experts, and timely feedback on the recommendation

## Concrete outcomes

1. **A 1-2 page recommendation document stating the decision, recommended scope, target users, success criteria, and what not to build yet:** This gives the sponsor a clear basis for a go or no-go decision and a tighter scope for the next delivery step.
2. **A one-page map of the selected journey step showing the 3-5 biggest breakdowns causing delay, confusion, or internal effort:** This makes visible where users or staff lose time, confidence, or momentum today.
3. **A simple concept view or 1-2 screen clickable prototype illustrating the recommended improvement:** This makes the recommended change tangible enough for review and early delivery planning.
4. **A short risk and next-step list covering assumptions, dependencies, and checks to complete before implementation:** This shows what still needs to be resolved before build starts.
