# Executive offer: Strategic UX and service design discovery

## What this is

When an organization is about to redesign a service, launch a portal, improve self-service, or modernize a workflow, the biggest risk is not moving too slowly. The biggest risk is moving into delivery before there is enough clarity on the real problem, the users affected, and the operational changes required for the solution to work.

This discovery offer gives leadership a stronger basis for what to invest in, what to delay, and what must change behind the interface before delivery begins.

## When this is relevant

This offer is relevant when:

* A redesign is being discussed, but the real problem is still loosely defined
* Teams disagree on priorities, scope, or the right direction
* A customer or employee journey spans several departments, systems, or handoffs
* Adoption, conversion, retention, or service efficiency is under pressure
* A major delivery investment needs a stronger strategic basis

## What leadership gets

The value of this work is not more UX activity. The value is:

* A clearer definition of the problem worth solving
* Better evidence on user needs, failure points, and decision moments
* Visibility into service, operational, and organizational dependencies
* A tested concept direction before broader delivery investment
* A practical recommendation on scope, sequencing, and next steps

## Engagement options

### Package A: Discovery sprint

* **Use when:** One bounded problem needs a fast go or no-go decision before development starts
* **Timeline:** 3-5 business days
* **Leadership gets:** A recommendation document, a map of the key breakdowns in the selected journey step, a simple concept view, and a short list of risks and next steps

### Package B: Service concept definition

* **Use when:** A product, portal, or service journey needs clearer user evidence, a future-state concept, and a tighter basis for scope
* **Timeline:** 2 weeks
* **Leadership gets:** An insight summary, current-state and future-state journey maps, a list of required service changes, a clickable prototype, and a prioritized delivery recommendation

### Package C: Strategic service redesign

* **Use when:** The initiative crosses departments, systems, or channels and requires a basis for investment, sequencing, and organizational change
* **Timeline:** Up to 4 weeks
* **Leadership gets:** An executive brief, a current-state service blueprint, a tested future-state service model, a phased roadmap, and a change and business case summary

## Why this pays off

The business case for discovery is simple:

* Fewer late scope changes once delivery starts
* Faster internal decisions on priorities and investment
* Better focus on the problems that matter most
* Lower rework and less wasted delivery effort
* Stronger adoption and service performance after implementation

## What happens next

The next step is a short scoping conversation to confirm:

* Which business decision this work needs to support
* Which users, journeys, or service areas matter most
* Which package matches the scale and risk of the problem

From there, we can recommend the right package and move directly into the discovery phase.
