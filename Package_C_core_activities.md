# Package C execution plan: Strategic service redesign

## Overview

**Timeline:** Up to 4 weeks
**Focus:** Define a future-state service model and investment roadmap for complex cross-functional change
**Best used when:** The initiative cuts across departments, systems, or channels and the client needs a basis for investment, sequencing, and organizational change

## Core activities

### Phase 1: Strategic framing and ecosystem alignment (Week 1)

* **Goal:** Define the strategic problem and align the initiative around the service, not just the screens
* **Activity 1.1: Conduct 5-7 executive and service-owner interviews, typically 45-60 minutes each, to clarify the business case, strategic priorities, and investment questions**
    * Use the interviews to understand where the current service model is under pressure and which decisions this work must support
    * Capture the outcomes, risks, and investment questions that matter to senior stakeholders
* **Activity 1.2: Run a half-day ecosystem workshop to agree the service scope across departments, touchpoints, systems, and operational actors**
    * Identify the departments, touchpoints, systems, and operational actors shaping the experience
    * Define where to focus the work so the study covers the critical service moments

### Phase 2: Fieldwork, data review, and current-state blueprint (Week 2)

* **Goal:** Understand how the service works in practice and where the real breakdowns occur
* **Activity 2.1: Conduct 5-8 contextual interviews, observations, or shadowing sessions, typically 60-90 minutes each, supported by a review of analytics, support patterns, and operational data**
    * Study how the service works in practice across channels, teams, and systems rather than relying on stated process alone
    * Capture workarounds, delays, ownership gaps, and hidden effort in the current service
* **Activity 2.2: Build a current-state service blueprint to expose delays, duplication, cost, and ownership gaps**
    * Map front-stage and back-stage interactions, supporting processes, and dependencies
    * Show where the current model creates friction, delay, duplication, cost, or lost value

### Phase 3: Target-state service concept and validation (Week 3)

* **Goal:** Define a future-state service direction and test whether it holds up with users and stakeholders
* **Activity 3.1: Run a half-day future-state service model workshop and validate the concept with 5-8 users, customers, or internal stakeholders**
    * Define how the future journey should work across channels, teams, and systems
    * Build and test a target-state concept with relevant users, customers, or internal stakeholders
    * Refine the model based on feedback, friction, and feasibility signals

### Phase 4: Roadmap, change implications, and business case support (Week 4)

* **Goal:** Convert the concept into a practical basis for prioritization and delivery planning
* **Activity 4.1: Run a half-day roadmap and business case session to create a phased roadmap, outline change implications, and frame the ROI hypothesis**
    * Identify what should happen now, next, and later and clarify dependencies across teams, systems, and operations
    * Summarize the operational, governance, and ownership changes required for the service to work
    * Connect the recommended direction to adoption, efficiency, support reduction, revenue, or retention outcomes and frame the ROI hypothesis for the next phase

## Client involvement

* **Commitment:** Approximately 3-4 days
* **Key action:** Participation from senior sponsors, service owners, and operational stakeholders at key checkpoints
* **Input needed:** Access to fieldwork, internal data, operational teams, and timely review of the future-state direction

## Concrete outcomes

1. **An executive brief summarizing the service problem, the value at stake, and the main investment questions:** This gives senior stakeholders a clear view of why the initiative matters and what decision the next phase must support.
2. **A current-state service blueprint showing where delays, duplication, costs, and ownership gaps damage the experience:** This makes visible where the service is leaking value across teams, systems, and handoffs.
3. **A tested future-state service model supported by concept feedback from 5-8 users, customers, or internal stakeholders:** This shows how the service should work across touchpoints, teams, and systems and whether the direction holds up in validation.
4. **A phased roadmap across teams and systems showing sequencing, dependencies, and major decision points:** This gives the client a practical basis for prioritization and staged delivery planning.
5. **A change and business case summary covering operating model implications, ownership changes, and the ROI hypothesis:** This shows what must change in the organization and why the investment is worth pursuing.
