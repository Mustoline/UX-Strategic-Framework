# Package B execution plan: Service concept definition

## Overview

**Timeline:** 2 weeks
**Focus:** Define the right concept for a product or journey before scope and priorities are locked in delivery
**Best used when:** The client knows the opportunity area, but still needs evidence on user needs, the future-state journey, and the supporting service changes

## Working model

Package B should still be run as a validated sequence. Each activity produces an output that should be reviewed before the next activity begins, so the concept direction, prototype, and recommendation stay tied to confirmed evidence rather than assumptions.

## Core activities

### Phase 1: Framing and recruitment (Days 1-2)

* **Goal:** Set the scope and prepare the research so the work answers the right commercial, user, and delivery questions

#### Activity 1.1: Scoping workshop and research frame

* **Estimated activity time:** 2 hours
* **Format:** Scoping workshop with the sponsor and key stakeholders
* **Participants:** Sponsor and relevant business, product, service, commercial, and delivery stakeholders
* **Purpose:** Agree the journey in scope, business case, success criteria, scope boundaries, and the questions the work must answer

#### Activity 1.2: Evidence review, recruitment, and user interviews

* **Estimated activity time:** 1-2 days across evidence prep, recruitment coordination, 4-6 interviews of 45-60 minutes each, and first synthesis
* **Format:** Evidence review, participant recruitment, and semi-structured user interview stream
* **Participants:** Delivery lead or researcher, target users, and evidence owners where needed
* **Purpose:** Build a grounded picture of user needs, barriers, and decision points before the work moves into journey and concept definition

### Phase 2: Research and journey mapping (Days 3-5)

* **Goal:** Understand the current experience from both the user and the service perspective

#### Activity 2.1: Current-state journey map and opportunity framing

* **Estimated activity time:** 4-6 hours of synthesis and mapping, plus a 45-60 minute stakeholder review checkpoint
* **Format:** Current-state journey mapping and synthesis readout
* **Participants:** Delivery lead or strategist, with stakeholder review from teams close to the journey and service reality
* **Purpose:** Show where the experience and service break down across users, teams, and systems and define the opportunity areas that matter most

### Phase 3: Concept definition and supporting service changes (Days 6-8)

* **Goal:** Translate insight into a future-state direction that can support delivery planning

#### Activity 3.1: Future-state concept working session

* **Estimated activity time:** 2-3 hours for the concept session, plus 3-5 hours of synthesis
* **Format:** Future-state concept working session and concept readout
* **Participants:** Relevant stakeholders across product, service, content, operations, ownership, and delivery
* **Purpose:** Define the future-state journey and the required changes in process, content, ownership, and data

### Phase 4: Prototype, prioritization, and recommendation (Days 9-10)

* **Goal:** Make the concept tangible and turn it into a practical delivery recommendation

#### Activity 4.1: Clickable prototype, prioritization workshop, and delivery recommendation

* **Estimated activity time:** 2 hours for the prioritization workshop, plus 1-2 days for prototype briefing, prompt generation, prototype production support, and recommendation packaging
* **Format:** Clickable prototype, prioritization workshop, and final recommendation pack
* **Participants:** Sponsor, relevant stakeholders, and delivery lead or strategist, with optional design support
* **Purpose:** Turn the future-state concept into a practical recommendation on what to build first, what to defer, and what still needs validation

## Client involvement

* **Commitment:** Approximately 1.5-2 days
* **Key action:** Participation in the scoping session, support for recruitment and user access, journey and concept reviews, prioritization workshop, and review checkpoints
* **Input needed:** Access to existing evidence, internal experts, target users or customer-facing teams, and timely feedback on the outputs

## How the outcomes are produced

* **Insight summary:** Built mainly in Activity 1.2 using the evidence review, interview notes, and synthesis of user needs, barriers, and decision criteria
* **Current-state and future-state journey maps:** Produced through Activity 2.1 and Activity 3.1, then refined for final delivery
* **List of required service changes:** Built mainly in Activity 3.1 from the future-state concept work across process, content, ownership, and data
* **Clickable prototype:** Shaped in Activity 4.1 through a clickable core-flow brief and a tool-ready prototype prompt pack, then built in the chosen design tool
* **Prioritized delivery recommendation:** Packaged in Activity 4.1 using the validated concept, prototype scope, prioritization logic, and remaining risks

The prototype prompt pack is an enabling artifact, not a substitute for the clickable prototype. Its role is to help generate a prototype that reflects the validated concept direction and the main learnings from the package.

## Concrete outcomes

1. **A concise insight summary from 4-6 interviews showing the top user needs, barriers, and decision criteria:** This gives the client a usable evidence base for the decisions ahead, not just raw research notes.
2. **A current-state and future-state journey map showing how the experience should change end to end:** This creates a shared view of what is broken today and what the future journey needs to do differently.
3. **A list of required service changes across process, content, ownership, and data, with the main dependencies called out:** This shows what has to change behind the scenes for the concept to work in practice.
4. **A clickable prototype of the core flow illustrating the proposed experience at the key decision points:** This makes the future-state direction tangible enough for stakeholder review and delivery planning.
5. **A prioritized delivery recommendation showing what to build first, what to defer, and what to validate next:** This turns the discovery work into a practical roadmap for the next phase.
