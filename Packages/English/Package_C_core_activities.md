# Package C execution plan: Strategic service redesign

## Overview

**Timeline:** Up to 4 weeks
**Focus:** Define a future-state service model and investment roadmap for complex cross-functional change
**Best used when:** The initiative cuts across departments, systems, or channels and the client needs a basis for investment, sequencing, and organizational change

## Working model

Package C should still be run as a validated sequence. Each activity produces an output that should be reviewed before the next activity begins, so the service model, roadmap, and business case stay tied to confirmed evidence rather than assumptions.

## Core activities

### Phase 1: Strategic framing and ecosystem alignment (Week 1)

* **Goal:** Define the strategic problem and align the initiative around the service, not just the screens

#### Activity 1.1: Executive and service-owner interviews

* **Estimated activity time:** 1-2 days across 5-7 interviews of 45-60 minutes each, plus first synthesis
* **Format:** Executive and service-owner interview stream
* **Participants:** Executives, service owners, and the delivery lead or strategist
* **Purpose:** Clarify the business case, strategic priorities, investment questions, and the outcomes that matter to senior stakeholders

#### Activity 1.2: Ecosystem workshop and service scope

* **Estimated activity time:** Half day
* **Format:** Ecosystem workshop with senior and operational stakeholders
* **Participants:** Senior sponsors, service owners, and operational stakeholders from the relevant parts of the service
* **Purpose:** Agree the service scope across departments, touchpoints, systems, and operational actors

### Phase 2: Fieldwork, data review, and current-state blueprint (Week 2)

* **Goal:** Understand how the service works in practice and where the real breakdowns occur

#### Activity 2.1: Contextual fieldwork and data review

* **Estimated activity time:** 3-5 days across 5-8 sessions of 60-90 minutes each, plus evidence review and first synthesis
* **Format:** Contextual interviews, observations, or shadowing supported by operational evidence
* **Participants:** Relevant users, customers, frontline staff, operational roles, and the delivery lead or strategist
* **Purpose:** Study how the service actually works across channels, teams, and systems rather than relying on stated process alone

#### Activity 2.2: Current-state service blueprint

* **Estimated activity time:** 1-2 days of blueprinting and synthesis, plus a 60-90 minute stakeholder review checkpoint
* **Format:** Current-state service blueprint and hotspot readout
* **Participants:** Delivery lead or strategist, with stakeholder review from teams close to the operating model
* **Purpose:** Expose delays, duplication, cost, and ownership gaps across front-stage and back-stage service delivery

### Phase 3: Target-state service concept and validation (Week 3)

* **Goal:** Define a future-state service direction and test whether it holds up with users and stakeholders

#### Activity 3.1: Future-state service model workshop and concept validation

* **Estimated activity time:** Half-day workshop plus 1-2 days for validation sessions, synthesis, and refinement
* **Format:** Future-state service model workshop followed by validation with 5-8 users, customers, or internal stakeholders
* **Participants:** Relevant cross-functional stakeholders plus validation participants who can test the future-state direction
* **Purpose:** Define how the future journey should work across channels, teams, and systems and test whether the direction holds up in practice

### Phase 4: Roadmap, change implications, and business case support (Week 4)

* **Goal:** Convert the concept into a practical basis for prioritization and delivery planning

#### Activity 4.1: Roadmap and business case session

* **Estimated activity time:** Half-day session plus 4-6 hours of synthesis and packaging
* **Format:** Roadmap and business case session with senior and operational stakeholders
* **Participants:** Senior sponsors, service owners, operational stakeholders, and the delivery lead or strategist
* **Purpose:** Create a phased roadmap, outline the change implications, and frame the ROI hypothesis for the next phase

## Client involvement

* **Commitment:** Approximately 3-4 days
* **Key action:** Participation from senior sponsors, service owners, operational stakeholders, and validation participants at each stage-gate review
* **Input needed:** Access to fieldwork, internal data, operational teams, relevant users or stakeholders, and timely review of the outputs

## How the outcomes are produced

* **Executive brief:** Built mainly in Activity 1.1 using the interview stream and the synthesis of strategic themes, service pressures, and investment questions
* **Current-state service blueprint:** Produced mainly in Activities 2.1 and 2.2 using fieldwork, operational evidence, and current-state blueprinting
* **Tested future-state service model:** Built in Activity 3.1 using the validated current-state blueprint, target-state workshop, and validation feedback
* **Phased roadmap:** Produced mainly in Activity 4.1 using the validated future-state model and the sequencing discussion
* **Change and business case summary:** Packaged in Activity 4.1 using the roadmap, change implications, dependencies, and ROI hypothesis

Where the tested future-state service model needs a more tangible validation aid, the package may also produce a prototype prompt pack for one selected high-risk journey slice. That support artifact should help generate a prototype in a design tool, but it does not replace the core Package C deliverables.

## Concrete outcomes

1. **An executive brief summarizing the service problem, the value at stake, and the main investment questions:** This gives senior stakeholders a clear view of why the initiative matters and what decision the next phase must support.
2. **A current-state service blueprint showing where delays, duplication, costs, and ownership gaps damage the experience:** This makes visible where the service is leaking value across teams, systems, and handoffs.
3. **A tested future-state service model supported by concept feedback from 5-8 users, customers, or internal stakeholders:** This shows how the service should work across touchpoints, teams, and systems and whether the direction holds up in validation.
4. **A phased roadmap across teams and systems showing sequencing, dependencies, and major decision points:** This gives the client a practical basis for prioritization and staged delivery planning.
5. **A change and business case summary covering operating model implications, ownership changes, and the ROI hypothesis:** This shows what must change in the organization and why the investment is worth pursuing.
