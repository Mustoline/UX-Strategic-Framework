# Package A execution plan: Discovery sprint

## Overview

**Timeline:** 5 business days
**Focus:** Make one near-term delivery decision with better evidence and less internal debate
**Best used when:** The client needs to decide whether and how to improve one specific feature area, workflow, or journey step before development begins

## Working model

Package A should still be run as a validated sequence. Each activity produces an output that should be reviewed before the next activity begins, so later recommendations are based on confirmed inputs rather than assumptions.

## Core activities

### Phase 1: Decision and evidence baseline (Days 1-2)

* **Goal:** Build a fast, shared understanding of the problem and the decision that needs to be made

#### Activity 1.1: Sponsor workshop and decision frame

* **Estimated activity time:** 90 minutes
* **Format:** Sponsor workshop with 3-5 decision-makers
* **Participants:** Sponsor and relevant business, product, service, or delivery stakeholders
* **Purpose:** Define the business question, target users, success criteria, and constraints for the sprint

#### Activity 1.2: Evidence baseline review

* **Estimated activity time:** 2-4 hours, depending on evidence quality and availability
* **Format:** Structured review of analytics, support issues, sales input, prior research, and other relevant evidence
* **Participants:** Delivery lead or strategist with access to evidence owners and relevant subject matter experts
* **Purpose:** Establish what is already known, what remains assumption, and which questions the sprint still needs to answer

#### Activity 1.3: Journey-step mapping session

* **Estimated activity time:** 60-90 minutes
* **Format:** Current-state mapping session of the selected journey step
* **Participants:** Relevant stakeholders close to the selected journey step, workflow, or feature area
* **Purpose:** Show where users or staff lose time, confidence, or momentum in the selected step

### Phase 2: Direction comparison (Day 3)

* **Goal:** Compare solution directions using shared criteria and narrow the recommendation to one preferred direction

#### Activity 2.1: Option review and preferred direction

* **Estimated activity time:** 2 hours
* **Format:** Structured option review with relevant stakeholders
* **Participants:** Sponsor plus stakeholders able to comment on user value, business impact, and delivery feasibility
* **Purpose:** Compare 1-2 realistic directions against agreed business, user, and delivery criteria

### Phase 3: Concept development and recommendation (Days 4-5)

* **Goal:** Turn the discovery work into a practical direction that can be used in delivery planning

#### Activity 3.1: Recommendation package and concept brief

* **Estimated activity time:** 4-8 hours of synthesis and packaging, plus a 30-45 minute sponsor review checkpoint
* **Format:** Recommendation document, simple concept view or prototype brief, and sponsor review
* **Participants:** Delivery lead or strategist, optional design support, and sponsor for final review
* **Purpose:** Convert the preferred direction into a practical next-step recommendation for delivery planning

## Client involvement

* **Commitment:** Approximately 4-6 hours
* **Key action:** Participation from the sponsor and relevant stakeholders in the sponsor workshop, mapping session, option review, and review checkpoints
* **Input needed:** Access to existing evidence, internal subject matter experts, and timely feedback on the outputs

## How the outcomes are produced

* **Recommendation document:** Built mainly in Activity 3.1 using the validated decision frame, evidence baseline, journey-step map, and preferred direction
* **Journey-step map:** Produced in Activity 1.3 and refined for final delivery in Activity 3.1
* **1-2 screen clickable prototype:** Shaped in Activity 3.1 through a concept brief and a tool-ready prototype prompt pack, then built in the chosen design tool
* **Risk and next-step list:** Packaged in Activity 3.1 using the open assumptions, dependencies, and implementation checks surfaced earlier in the sprint

The prototype prompt pack is an enabling artifact, not a substitute for the clickable prototype. Its role is to help generate a prototype that reflects the validated decisions and learnings from the sprint.

## Concrete outcomes

1. **A 1-2 page recommendation document stating the decision, recommended scope, target users, success criteria, and what not to build yet:** This gives the sponsor a clear basis for a go or no-go decision and a tighter scope for the next delivery step.
2. **A one-page map of the selected journey step showing the 3-5 biggest breakdowns causing delay, confusion, or internal effort:** This makes visible where users or staff lose time, confidence, or momentum today.
3. **A simple concept view or 1-2 screen clickable prototype illustrating the recommended improvement:** This makes the recommended change tangible enough for review and early delivery planning.
4. **A short risk and next-step list covering assumptions, dependencies, and checks to complete before implementation:** This shows what still needs to be resolved before build starts.
